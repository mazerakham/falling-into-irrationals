How to read this

You're falling, forever, toward a single point on the number line — an irrational number, which by definition no fraction ever lands on exactly. Every vertical line is a fraction p/q. The brighter it burns, the better that fraction pins the number at this depth, and each beam is coloured by which side it's on — red to the left of the target, blue to the right. The convergents — the number's all-time best approximations, the ones we actually use — are the beams that glow and carry a label as you fall past. (That blaze at 355/113 is the classic approximation of π, right to seven decimals.)

Watch them alternate. The convergents always straddle the target — one just above, the next just below, each closer than the last, never two in a row on the same side. The dim ladders between flares are the near-misses climbing toward the next record.

Some numbers won't be caught. The golden ratio φ, √2, and √5 are the stubborn ones: their flares are evenly spaced and never especially bright, because they are the hardest numbers in existence to approximate with fractions. e approaches in gentle, widening pulses. π is the wild one — long stretches of near-darkness, then a colossal flare, because its continued fraction hides one freakishly large term that makes 355/113 almost suspiciously good.
